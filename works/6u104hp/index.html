Overview 6U104HP is an aluminium Eurorack power case, 6/7U high and 104HP wide: it powers a modular system and provides the I/O and mounting hardware around it.

We left a multifunction strip above and below the module rows: ten 1/8'' and 1/4'' TRS jacks with built-in converters (currently passive, convertible to active); five 2-6 mults (convertible to active mults, mixers, S&H, logic and so on); two XLR sockets for gooseneck lamps (convertible to USB and other uses); and a cascadable power output.

The supply takes a 12–36V wide input; the body and lid are aluminium; the carbon-fibre side panels take single- or dual-case stands, and the stands are carbon fibre too, so they weigh almost nothing.

The 75×75 VESA holes on the back mean the case does not have to sit on a desk — it can be held up by a robot arm, or mounted on the adjustable VESA stands commonly used for portable monitors.

This case is the basis of most of my work — without it I could not have made many of my pieces and performances.

From customer to co-developer: three versions The starting point was simple: when I first got into Eurorack I wanted a case that was affordable and good to use, but the ones on the market were either very expensive or not very good. So I asked Ciiyte (Cai Yitong) to design one for me.

The first version was made of extremely thick anodised aluminium. Several problems only appeared once it arrived: the natural finish of JLCPCB's anodised aluminium looked terrible; it was heavy — a single empty 7U104HP case weighed 3.5kg; the stand would not fit at all, because of a design error; and there was no lid yet. I received the case at school, shipped directly from JLCPCB together with crude, custom-length CPCI-style rails, and assembled it myself with a senior schoolmate, turning the screws by hand. Everything about it was crude and wild.

For the second version we abandoned anodising for powder coating and added a lid, which improved the feel considerably. But the stand was still an insert type — long stand tabs slotted directly into the case's side panels; the empty case still weighed 3.5kg, the rails were still very heavy, and the side panels were 7-series aluminium. At that time my case was a 7U104 with the I/O on the 1U row, including MIDI I/O. Later I found that I did not need MIDI I/O at all.

Around this time Xuanning designed Asian Rail, a very light aluminium weight-saving rail. The third version adopted it, and finally made use of the upper and lower strips instead of taking space from the module rows. The stand also changed from an insert type to a quick-release design fixed to the sides with brackets.

We saved weight one gram at a time — at that point we were weighing even the internal circuit boards and the connectors. I myself gave up 7U to save weight; another reason was that six 7U104 cases would give me six 104HP 1U rows, and there was no realistic way I would fill them.

The result: the first version's empty case weighed 3.5kg; most of our cases now weigh 3.5kg fully loaded, with the lid on.

Thanks to Ciiyte (Cai Yitong) and FunnyCoco (Deng Jianbo) — none of this would exist without them.

The gooseneck lamp sockets were my proposal, and they came out of one specific performance. In 2023 Dawless Teahouse held an event at System; I performed with Tredd, YK3328 and Tang Zhengyi as ShangTide on the Danlu stage. That night every light was switched off and it was pitch black, and we could hardly ask the venue to light our gear — modular synths are too complex for that; without light we had no idea what our hands were doing. The same thing happened several more times, so we decided to design these lamps into the case. The lamps originally used BNC connectors, but they swivelled around during performances — unbearable — so we switched to XLR.
